භේරුණ්ඩ පක්ෂියා Bherunda Pakshiya

A British railway pushes into the Kandyan hills, and a house that has never needed it begins to come apart.

Sri Lanka, 2006 Sinhala, colour Written and directed by Sarath Dharmasiri Adapted from his novel of the same name

කතාවThe story

The British are cutting a railway line from Colombo up into Kandy. In the villages along the survey, the plan is not merely difficult; it is unthinkable. Hills are not there to be opened.

At the centre of the film stands Pitangane, a Sinhala man of enormous local authority, whose world runs on inherited belief, ritual and the word of elders. Against him runs the line itself, and the engineer who has come to build it — a man with instruments, a gradient and a completion date.

When the engineer falls in love with a village girl, the argument stops being about a railway. It moves indoors, into a household, and the two orders that had been circling each other are forced to share a roof.

The film is Dharmasiri's own adaptation of his novel Bherunda Pakshiya, which won the Sarathchandra Abhinandana award. It was his second feature. Premasiri Khemadasa wrote the score; Dinesh Kumara shot it.

ගැටුමWhere the film sets its argument

What the village knows

Charms and chants, the authority of a headman, the reading of auspicious times, a house held together by what the elders have always said. Knowledge arrives by inheritance and is not up for revision.

What the survey knows

Theodolite and chain, gradients, cuttings and a date by which the line must reach Kandy. Knowledge arrives by measurement, and a mountain is only a problem that has not yet been costed.

මුල් නවකතාවThe novel it came from

Before it was a film, Bherunda Pakshiya was a novel — written by Sarath Dharmasiri and published in Sinhala by Godage International Publishers. It won the Sarathchandra Abhinandana award, and Dharmasiri then adapted his own book for the screen, writing the script as well as directing.

The novel is still in print. If you want the story in the form it was first told, this is where to start.

නිර්මාණයOn location

Dharmasiri came to the film after years of writing for the screen and for television, and this was his second feature as a director. The production worked on location and on film — jungle, verandahs, temple courtyards, a small crew and an Arriflex.

The score was written by Premasiri Khemadasa, the composer who more or less remade Sinhala film music from the 1960s onward. He died in 2008, two years after the film's release; it is among the last of the more than 150 scores he wrote.
